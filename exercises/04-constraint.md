# Exercise 4: Find and Work the Constraint

**Time:** 15 minutes  
**After:** Tea shop at rush hour / Theory of Constraints

**Theory of Constraints** (Goldratt): One bottleneck limits your system.

## Five Focusing Steps

1. **IDENTIFY** the constraint
2. **EXPLOIT** it (cheap wins)
3. **SUBORDINATE** everything to it
4. **ELEVATE** it (expensive, long-term)
5. **REPEAT**

## Your Task

### Step 1: Identify (5 min)

**Is the constraint outside or inside?**
- Outside (no customers) → Move up visibility (marketing)
- Inside (can't deliver) → Find the bottleneck

For Harbour Insurance: **Inside** (demand exists, can't deliver)

**Where on your map?**
- Where work piles up (3-day PR wait)
- Where specialists are overloaded (2 seniors + architect)
- What limits throughput (review capacity)

**Mark the primary constraint** with a striped bar.

### Step 2: Mark Inertia (2 min)

**Constraint ≠ Inertia**
- Constraint = capacity limit
- Inertia = resistance to change

**Mark one key inertia** with a solid bar.

Common: Domain knowledge in heads, "only seniors can review"

### Step 3: Exploit Before Elevate (8 min)

**EXPLOIT (cheap, do first):**
- Small PRs (easier to review)
- Review before new work (social rule)
- Ensemble programming (review during coding)
- AI-generated review comments
- Document decisions as made

**SUBORDINATE (align to constraint):**
- Don't start new work if PRs wait
- AI creates small PRs
- Specs clear enough for less scrutiny

**ELEVATE (expensive, only if needed):**
- Train more reviewers
- Architecture as code
- Hire seniors

**Where does the constraint move next?**
Mark likely next bottleneck: Architecture decisions? Domain knowledge?

## Discussion

1. What's your primary constraint?
2. Best exploit move (start Monday, no budget)?
3. Where does it move after you fix this?
