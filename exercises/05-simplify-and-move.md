# Exercise 5: Simplify and move

**Time:** about 20 minutes
**After:** strategy and the OODA loop, and knowledge architecture

## Background: one doctrine, use appropriate methods

One way of working does not fit the whole map. Pick the way of working by the stage each component is in, as you placed it in Exercise 3. This helps when you choose your move.

| | Genesis, custom built | Product | Commodity, utility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The goal | Learn fast | Get it right | Cut cost and variation |
| How we work | Agile: small experiments | Lean: improve the flow | Six Sigma: standardise and automate |
| How we get it | Build it ourselves | Buy it and fit it in | Rent it as a utility |

## First: cut it down

Your map probably has 15 to 25 components by now. That is too many for one decision.

1. Write the question your map answers at the top. "How do we build software?" is too broad. "Why hasn't AI coding made delivery faster?" works.
2. Trace the chain from the need to the constraint. Everything on that chain stays.
3. Remove every component that does not change the answer.
4. Aim for 12 components or fewer.

Four ways to make a map readable:

- One question per map: keep what changes the answer.
- One map per user, when their needs really differ.
- Map around the constraint: the chain to it, nothing else.
- Fold a cluster into a submap, and mark it with a double ring.

## Then: make a move

1. Draw one strategic move on your simplified map. Use a dashed arrow for natural evolution, which happens anyway. Use a solid arrow for a move you intend to push.
2. Pick a gameplay pattern that speeds the move up or holds it back:
   - Open approaches: commoditise a layer someone else owns.
   - Innovate, leverage, commoditise.
   - Standards game: set the rules others build on.
   - Exploit inertia: move where a rival cannot follow.
   - Co-creation: build it with users, not for them.
   - Education: train more people, so you depend less on a few experts.
3. Ask who loses if the move succeeds. That is where you will meet inertia.

Some moves that fit Harbour Insurance:

| Move | Who might resist | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Review moves from a few seniors to the whole team, with automated checks | Senior developers | They lose their role as gatekeepers |
| Architecture rules become code and automated checks | The architect | They lose direct control |
| Domain knowledge is written down and kept up to date | Domain experts | Their knowledge becomes less scarce |

## Back together

- Is the story clearer after cutting the map down?
- Which component did you move, and where does it end up?
- Who gains from that move, and who loses?
- Would you dare to make that move this quarter?

## After the evening

Take this back to your team and map something real. The map is not the territory. It is a tool for a better conversation.
