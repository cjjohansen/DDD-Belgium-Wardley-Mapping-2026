# Sun Tzu's five factors & Boyd's OODA loop — source material for concept slides

Research notes for a DDD Belgium Wardley Mapping workshop. All quotes verified against the linked sources. Slide placement (upfront intro vs deeper section) is undecided — this file only gathers material.

## 1. Sun Tzu's five factors (Wardley's version)

### The original five (The Art of War, ch. 1 "Laying Plans", Giles translation)

"The art of war, then, is governed by five constant factors... These are: (1) The Moral Law; (2) Heaven; (3) Earth; (4) The Commander; (5) Method and discipline."

- Moral Law — the people in complete accord with their ruler
- Heaven — night and day, cold and heat, times and seasons
- Earth — distances, danger and security, open ground and narrow passes
- The Commander — wisdom, sincerity, benevolence, courage, strictness
- Method and discipline — marshalling of the army, ranks, supply, expenditure

Primary free texts: https://ctext.org/art-of-war/laying-plans | https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sun-tzu/works/art-of-war/ch01.htm | https://classics.mit.edu/Tzu/artwar.html

### Wardley's translation into the strategy cycle

Wardley (ch. 1 "On being lost") re-reads the same five as: **purpose, landscape, climate, doctrine, leadership**, drawn as a circle (the strategy cycle). The semantic mapping follows from his own definitions:

| Sun Tzu (ch. 1) | Wardley | Wardley's gloss (paraphrased from "On being lost") |
|---|---|---|
| Moral Law | **Purpose** | "your moral imperative... the reason why others follow you" |
| Heaven | **Climate** | "the forces that act upon the environment... the patterns of the seasons and the rules of the game" |
| Earth | **Landscape** | "the environment that you compete in... the features of the landscape including any obstacles in your way" |
| The Commander | **Leadership** | "the strategy that you choose... It is to 'the battle at hand'. It is context specific" |
| Method and discipline | **Doctrine** | "the training of your forces, the standard ways of operating and the techniques that you almost always apply" |

Note the reorder: Wardley runs the cycle purpose → landscape → climate → doctrine → leadership (Earth before Heaven). His key diagnosis: most strategies jump straight from purpose to leadership and skip the middle three.

Sources: Wardley, "On being lost" (blog) https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/08/on-being-lost.html (Medium version: https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec; one-file book PDF: https://www.swardleymaps.com/docs/wardley-maps-book.pdf). learnwardleymapping.com intro: "Sun Tzu's five factors — Purpose, Landscape, Climate, Doctrine, and Leadership — covered the minimum set of considerations required for strategic decision-making." https://learnwardleymapping.com/introduction/ (and https://archive.learnwardleymapping.com/). Erik Schön, *The Art of Strategy* (free on Medium, CC BY-SA 4.0) — a chapter-by-chapter reading of Sun Tzu through Boyd and Wardley: https://medium.com/illumination/the-art-of-strategy-6a24ed56bba7 (glossary with "Strategy Cycle" entry: https://medium.com/illumination/the-art-of-strategy-491606bebb27).

## 2. Boyd's OODA loop

### Canonical description and intent

- **Observe – Orient – Decide – Act**: a decision-making model by USAF Col. John Boyd (early 1970s), applied to combat operations and later to organisations. Continuous feedback; commitment comes late (unlike PDCA). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OODA_loop
- USMC doctrine MCDP-6 gives the clean textbook walkthrough: observe the situation, orient (make estimates and judgments to create a mental image), decide, act — "Having acted, we have changed the situation, and so the cycle begins again." https://irp.fas.org/doddir/usmc/mcdp6/ch2.htm
- **Boyd's intent — tempo and getting inside the opponent's loop**: an entity that cycles "more rapidly and/or more effectively than an opponent, can thereby get inside the opponent's decision cycle and gain the advantage" (Wikipedia, above). Military framing: "tighten" your loop, "loosen" the adversary's (Fadok, Air University Press).

### Link to Wardley's strategy cycle

Wardley built the strategy cycle by marrying Sun Tzu's five factors to Boyd's OODA: "To cope with this iterative process I have found that the work of... John Boyd... is most appropriate... I've married together both Sun Tzu and John Boyd to create a strategy cycle." (https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/08/on-being-lost.html). In the BoS talk he makes the correspondence explicit: "This overlaps with something else I read from John Boyd... talks about the OODA loop. So you have the game, your purpose, the first thing you need to do is observe the environment" — i.e. landscape + climate serve the observe/orient half, doctrine and leadership the decide/act half (https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/mapping-the-future-of-your-business). Schön's glossary puts the mapping-as-orientation point plainly: "High situational awareness comes from skillful use of the OODA loop, e.g. using Wardley Mapping." (https://medium.com/illumination/the-art-of-strategy-491606bebb27).

### The nuance: OODA is not a simple circle

Boyd's own expanded diagram makes **Orientation the hub** — it shapes observation, decision and action, and is shaped by feedback; there are implicit direct paths from orientation to observation and to action. Sources: Boyd's "Essence of Winning and Losing" slides ("Note how orientation shapes observation, shapes decision, shapes action", scan at USMCU https://www.usmcu.edu/Portals/218/HD/Digitized%20Collections/COLL_2062%20John%20Boyd/ — reference only, rebuild, don't copy); Chet Richards, "Boyd's OODA Loop" https://ooda.de/media/chet_richards_-_boyds_ooda_loop.pdf; DTIC "Control Warfare: Inside the OODA Loop" https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA391774.pdf; readable modern write-up: https://strategyu.co/ooda-loop.

## 3. How others teach it

- **Simon Wardley, "Mapping the Future of your Business" (Business of Software)** — origin story (two translations of Sun Tzu), names the five factors, then says it "overlaps with" Boyd's OODA and maps purpose/landscape/climate onto observe/orient. The single best teacher's script for these two slides. https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/mapping-the-future-of-your-business
- **Simon Wardley, "Crossing the River by Feeling the Stones" (keynote)** — has an explicit chapter "Five Factors That Mattered in Competition" at 4:46, then situational awareness and maps. Good pacing model: factors first, map second. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xlNYYy8pzB4
- **Erik Schön, GOTO 2020 interview on *The Art of Strategy*** — draws the OODA loop live and boils Boyd down to observe-orient first, then "implementing the feedback loop better than the competition". Sun Tzu + Boyd + Wardley in one narrative. https://gotopia.tech/episodes/17/how-to-be-certain-you-will-succeed-interview-with-erik-schon (video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-z3ZB1ck_nk)
- **learnwardleymapping.com** — the free book is structured exactly as the five factors (1. Purpose, 2. Landscape, 3. Climate, 4. Doctrine, 5. Leadership); the intro derives them from Sun Tzu before any map mechanics. A model of "concepts upfront, tool after". https://learnwardleymapping.com/introduction/
- **Susanne Kaiser** — teaches the strategy cycle section-by-section (purpose → landscape → climate → doctrine → leadership/gameplay) as the backbone of her "Architecture for Flow" combination of Wardley Mapping + DDD + Team Topologies — directly relevant to a DDD audience. InfoQ talk: https://www.infoq.com/presentations/ddd-wardley-mapping-team-topology ; podcast: https://blog.container-solutions.com/susanne-kaiser-wardley-mapping-domain-driven-design-team-topologies
- **Schön's Art of Strategy glossary (Medium, CC BY-SA 4.0)** — the "Strategy Cycle" entry explicitly combines Sun Tzu's five factors, Boyd's OODA and the two types of "why" in one definition; useful wording crib. https://medium.com/illumination/the-art-of-strategy-491606bebb27

## 4. Slide-material suggestions

### Slide A — Sun Tzu's five factors (Wardley's version)

- Purpose → Landscape → Climate → Doctrine → Leadership — Sun Tzu's five, reordered as a cycle
- Skip the middle three and you get strategy by gut feel
- Mapping exists to fill the landscape gap

(~30 words)

### Slide B — Boyd's OODA loop and the strategy cycle

- Observe, Orient, Decide, Act — a loop, not a project plan
- Orientation is the hub: it shapes how you see and act
- Wardley mapping is the orientation aid; cycling faster than change keeps strategy honest

(~34 words)
